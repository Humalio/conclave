# Shares, channels and the bridge

[Shares](../server/src/shares/) hold a version manifest and supported file blobs. Inline uploads enter a draft; `final: true` publishes it, and `from_version` seeds a draft. A bridge upload, or a channel save into a share, publishes only its own snapshot: the files it received, and nothing from a draft. A draft that is open at the time is left as it was, with its files and note, and moves to the next version number, so the published versions stay in order and the draft still publishes later with `final: true`. Recipients may be agents, rooms, or teams. On reads, recheck the share state, current membership, and the author-to-recipient messaging rules. Feedback is scoped to a version; remap anchors where the later version still supports them, or mark them outdated. Promotion to the vault runs per file, checks vault permissions and requires a supplied base revision for an overwrite. Share metadata may exist for oversized files whose content was not retained.

[Channels](../server/src/channels/) require `channel.use`; agent targets also require peer grants and explicit acceptance. Inline mode uses ordered base64 chunks with a bounded unread window; honor backpressure and verify size and hash. It cannot resume after a hub restart. Vault and share targets commit through their normal consistency paths. A vault save is authorized again when it is saved, not only when the channel opened, under the write lock just before the bytes land: the sender's key and the app key it opened the channel through must still be active, none of the sender's teams may be paused or switched off by the operator, and the key's rules and temporary grants, narrowed by that app key's cap, must still grant `fs.create` (a new file) or `fs.update` (with a base revision) on the target, plus `channel.use` for a channel opened with `channel_open`. Otherwise nothing is written, the refusal is logged as a denied access, and the channel ends as `failed` with `failure_reason: "denied"` (`"paused"` for a paused team). An agent, PC, all-writes or vault lockdown also stops the save (`"lockdown"`). A share upload is authorized again at publication, on every path: `share_upload` with inline files, a channel save into a share and a bridge upload. After the files are staged and just before the version is published, the share must still be open, no lockdown may stop the write, the uploader's key and the app key it came through must still be active, none of the uploader's teams may be paused, and the key's rules and temporary grants, narrowed by that app key's cap, must still grant `share.upload` on `share:<id>`. Otherwise nothing is published, the refusal is logged as a denied access, and the call fails, or the channel ends as `failed` with `failure_reason: "denied"` (`"paused"`, `"lockdown"`). A bridge or inline channel upload into the vault or a share is also checked before each chunk is staged, so a grant revoked mid-transfer stops it at the next chunk and its staging file is deleted. A channel that the sender closes before publication publishes nothing. An agent-to-agent channel keeps relaying only while the grants that opened it hold: the sender still has `channel.use` and `channel.open` on the receiver, and the receiver still has `channel.use` and `channel.accept` for the sender (a rule changed or a team link removed after the offer was accepted). Each chunk, each `channel_receive` and the hub's sweep check this, and a channel that fails it ends as `failed` with `failure_reason: "denied"`. A download is authorized again for every chunk it delivers, inline or bridge, as the tool call that requested it was: the reader's key and the app key it requested the download through must both still be active, and the key's rules and temporary grants, narrowed by that app key's cap, must still grant `fs.read` and `fs.download` on the vault file, or `share.read` on the share (the share's author and the reviewer of a task in review that names it need no grant, as when they requested it), and the share must still be open to the reader. So a revoked or suspended app key, or a cap narrowed between the request and the delivery, stops the download at its next chunk. Otherwise the channel ends as `failed` with `failure_reason: "denied"` and no further bytes go out. A channel whose key is suspended or revoked is also failed the next time the hub sweeps channels (on any channel open, accept or bridge poll), and the sweep expires unaccepted offers. An open transfer that no one touches for two hours (no chunk, no status call from its bridge, no `channel_send` or `channel_receive`) is abandoned: the sweep ends it as `expired` with `failure_reason: "idle"`, deletes its staging file and frees its slot, so a new upload to the same share or file can open at once. Every call on the channel restarts the two hours. A hub restart restarts them too for every bridge transfer still open, so a transfer that was about to resume is not ended as idle because the hub was down. At startup the hub also deletes staging files whose channel is no longer open. A relay keeps bounded bytes in memory; uploads to persistent destinations may use staging files.

## Bridge protocol

The bridge is a Node.js program that polls outbound, scoped to one PC. An operator issues a PC setup key. The setup tools supply installation instructions, program parts and a hash, and a short-lived pairing code. Pairing returns a bridge credential. The operator approves folders. Hub and bridge both check authorized paths, and the bridge validates the entire folder manifest before publishing individual files. See the [bridge operator guide](../bridge/README.md).

The connector's service is the bridge. It authenticates with the PC's app key, so there is no separate pairing. The operator still approves each folder, and hub and connector both check authorized paths. The hub accepts the app key on every `/bridge/` route except `/bridge/pair`, and refuses it there as it refuses the bridge key when the PC is suspended. The stand-alone bridge with its own bridge key stays for PCs without the connector until the connector's pairing is in force ([connector](connector.md#file-transfers)).

- `POST /bridge/pair` exchanges the pairing code; `POST /bridge/poll` reports heartbeat and asks for authorized jobs.
- `GET /bridge/channels/:id` reports offsets and status. Chunk PUT/GET calls advance at explicit offsets and return expected offsets or backpressure when applicable. A restarted relay resumes from the receiver's confirmed offset.
- The folder stream uses a big-endian 32-bit header length, a JSON header with relative path and byte size, then file bytes; zero header length terminates it. Raw vault transfers use file bytes. The sender skips symlinks, common credential/dependency paths and matching ignore rules.
- Completion uses the `done` endpoint. The receiving bridge confirms local publication after verification, then the hub reports completion. An open, verified receive remains eligible for delivery until the hub records that confirmation. Hash agreement checks integrity. Text inspection is bounded and binary transfers can contain secrets.

## Task files from the PC

`task_create`, `task_update` and `task_submit` take `local_paths` (or `local_path` for one file): files on the agent's PC,
attached without their bytes passing through the call. The PC's connector reads them and uploads them through the
bridge routes, and the hub writes them into the task's brief or submission files when the upload ends.

- **Which PC:** a session logged in through a PC's app key, whose connector has checked in (or whose stand-alone bridge is
  paired). With neither, the call fails with `bridge_required` and says to use the connector or inline attachments.
- **Which files:** only files inside the calling agent's own folder, as the connector registered it for that agent on this
  hub; never another folder of the PC, even one the operator approved. The hub names the agent on the send job, and the
  connector resolves the real path (links and junctions followed) and checks it against that folder's real path. A path
  outside it, or a link pointing outside it, fails the upload with a message saying where files may come from.
- **Refused:** `.git` and `.codex` folders, the role file at the folder's root (`CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`,
  `AGENTS.override.md`) and the connector's own folders. The hub refuses these names at once; the connector checks the real
  path again. A folder upload skips `.git`, `.codex` and `node_modules`, and leaves out the role files (`CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `AGENTS.override.md`) at the root of the folder instead of failing the whole upload (a role file sent on its own is refused). The refused names are refused under their Windows spellings too: `.git.` and `.git ` (trailing dot or space), the short names `GIT~1` and `CODEX~1`, an alternate stream, and the device names `CON`, `PRN`, `AUX`, `NUL`, `COM1` to `COM9` and `LPT1` to `LPT9`, on send and on receive; the hub checks the name on send, the connector checks it and the real path again. A file received for one agent is also refused
  when its `save_to` (or the real path behind a link or junction) lies inside the folder of another agent registered on
  this PC for the same hub. The receive job carries the receiving agent's id and label, and a send job carries the sending agent's id and label, so the folders a send may read are matched by id in the same way. The PC tells agents apart by id: each
  binding records the hub's agent id at setup or restore (or the id its relay recorded), so two teams on one PC that each have a
  Coordinator, or an agent the PC labelled `Coordinator-2` after a clash, are never confused. A binding whose id this PC does not know (made
  before ids were recorded, and no relay has run) counts as another agent's folder for a job that names its receiver by id, so
  the receiver's own old folder is refused until it is registered again, which records the id (safe: labels can be shared by
  two teams). Labels decide only for a job with no agent id (an older hub); a job with neither fails closed: every agent folder
  counts as foreign. Hub and connector go out together in 0.9.6. `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md` are refused by name in any folder, in any letter case.
- **Checks:** the inline rules apply as the files land: names, types sniffed from the bytes, one file at most 8 MB, an HTML
  mockup at most 2 MB, and the set, task and team limits. Text goes through the leak guard. The task is checked again before
  each chunk and as the files land: it has not ended, the agent may still change that set, no lockdown and no pause.
- **Result:** the set's next version. A brief merges onto its previous version; a submission's files make a new round, and
  while the task is in review on that submission, the submission names that round. A refusal fails the channel with its reason.
- **State on the task:** `task_list` with `task_id` (or `detail: "full"`) lists the task's uploads from the PC, newest first,
  each with its state, to everyone who may already read the task's files (its creator, assignee and reviewer while it is live,
  and the team's coordinators) and to no one else:
  - **uploading**, with the file names;
  - **received**, with each file's name, size and sha256, computed by the hub from the bytes it stored;
  - **failed**, with a reason in the hub's own fixed words (too large, refused name or type, task ended or changed hands,
    lockdown, paused team, stopped and timed out, closed, hub restarted). An upload that stalls fails when its channel
    expires, so none stays "uploading" for ever.

  The file names are the agent's text (L2); no channel id, upload token or PC path is shown. The reviewer reads the same
  list, and the task says when a submission's files are still uploading or failed, so it is not approved before they arrive.
  Keep the staged copy in your folder until its upload says **received**; then it can be removed. `channel_status` still
  answers the agent that made the call for its own uploads (any other channel needs `tool:channel_status` and `channel.use`).
- **Audit:** each upload is one channel of one agent and one call: `task.attach_local` at the call (the channel and how
  many paths, never the paths), and `task.attach` with the channel when the files land.
- Local paths go alone: not with `attachments`, `attachments_from` or a `share_id` in the same call.

Use [bridge HTTP handlers](../server/src/bridge/http.ts), [frame codec](../server/src/util/frames.ts), and the [MCP transfer schemas](../server/src/mcp/channelTools.ts) for exact fields, limits and error behavior. See [risk register](risk-register.md) for remaining transfer risks.

## Default limits

Inline channels allow 1 MiB total. Bridge channels allow up to 20 GiB per transfer. The hub permits four active channels per key and 32 globally, with a default 50 GiB hourly channel allowance per key and an 8 MiB relay buffer. Unaccepted offers expire after ten minutes; open transfers expire after two hours without activity. Share uploads default to 1 GiB per key per hour; an inline `share_upload` that is refused or fails publishes nothing and is not charged. Relayed bytes are visible to the hub, so the bridge protocol does not provide peer-to-peer end-to-end encryption. See [application defaults](../server/src/app.ts) and [configuration](../server/src/config.ts).
